What this tool does

First, identify how delivery should work: extra people you manage, a supplier-led project, an ongoing managed service, or a standard service you consume. Then examine what each party must invest to make the relationship succeed.

The theoretical background

Incomplete-contracts theory: a contract cannot specify and enforce every future contribution. Important learning, initiative and improvements may therefore depend on incentives beyond the written obligations.

The property-rights approach of Grossman, Hart and Moore explains how ownership changes bargaining alternatives and incentives to invest. Stronger control for one party can strengthen its investment incentive while weakening the other’s. Reusability helps us examine outside options; it does not by itself determine ownership.

The four service models come from ITPF slide 61. Their selection uses practical delivery rules—not a formula derived from ownership theory. The theory informs the separate investment advice. Choose “Not sure yet” when evidence is missing.

How should both parties benefit?

Performance benefits can include faster resolution, better availability, fewer errors or higher service quality. Agree a baseline and measurable improvement targets.

Sharing savings, revenue or performance benefits does not require a JV. Consider a JV only where joint ownership and governance add value beyond a contract.

Decision rules, source and limits

The four model names and commercial starting points follow ITPF slide 61, “Types of IT Outsourcing Services”. The selection rules are prototype judgement rules; the slide does not supply an automated selection algorithm.

Ownership decision path: assets with little useful value apart → assess unified ownership; independently useful assets → start with separate ownership. For unified ownership, identify whose critical investment benefits most from control. If both are critical, compare either party as owner. Assess benefit sharing separately.

Benefit-sharing decision path: where both parties need an improvement incentive, start with gainsharing for measurable savings or revenue sharing for attributable additional revenue. For measurable performance improvements, agree KPI-based incentives. Combine these with financial benefit sharing where appropriate and avoid rewarding the same improvement twice. Where none is measurable, establish a measurement basis or use agreed milestones. Consider a JV only if joint control is needed and contractual governance is insufficient. These are practical commercial rules, not deductions of an optimal contract from PRT.

Property-rights reasoning is applied separately: ownership affects bargaining alternatives and incentives for investments that cannot be fully contracted for. Reuse and cost recovery are relevant, but neither alone establishes the best ownership arrangement. This tool does not estimate investment responses or calculate an economically optimal allocation.

SLAs, asset ownership and delivery accountability are distinct. As-a-Service can coexist with Managed Services: assess each scope separately. Model labels overlap in supplier marketing; agreed responsibilities take precedence. Relative cost and mobilisation descriptions on the slide are contextual, not universal rankings. This prototype does not calculate prices or assume immediate deployment.

You retain strategy, risk acceptance and supplier governance under every model. The SAP example does not establish your RISE contractual responsibility matrix.
